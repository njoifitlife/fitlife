Workout Plans

Choose your level. Every plan is designed for women over 40, with modifications for joints, energy levels, and time constraints.

Beginner — Building Your Foundation

3 days per week, 20-30 minutes. No equipment needed.

Day 1 — Lower Body Strength

  • Bodyweight Squats 3 sets x 10 reps

    Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Lower hips back and down as if sitting in a chair. Keep chest up. Modification: Use a chair behind you for support.

  • Glute Bridges 3 sets x 12 reps

    Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Push hips up, squeezing glutes at the top. Great for lower back and hip strength.

  • Standing Calf Raises 3 sets x 15 reps

    Rise up on your toes, hold for a second, then lower. Hold a wall for balance if needed.

  • Wall Sit 3 sets x 20 seconds

    Lean against a wall with thighs parallel to the floor. Build up duration over time.

Day 2 — Upper Body & Core

  • Wall Push-Ups 3 sets x 10 reps

    Stand arm's length from a wall. Place hands on wall and push. Progress to incline push-ups on a counter as you get stronger.

  • Seated Arm Circles 3 sets x 30 seconds

    Extend arms to the side, make small circles. Great for shoulder mobility and warming up joints.

  • Dead Bug 3 sets x 8 reps each side

    Lie on your back, arms up, knees at 90 degrees. Lower opposite arm and leg while keeping your back flat on the floor.

  • Bird Dog 3 sets x 8 reps each side

    On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg. Builds core stability and balance.

Day 3 — Full Body & Flexibility

  • March in Place 3 minutes warm-up

    Lift knees high, pump arms. Gets the heart rate up gently.

  • Step-Ups 3 sets x 8 reps each leg

    Use a sturdy step or the bottom stair. Hold a railing for balance.

  • Standing Side Leg Lifts 3 sets x 10 reps each side

    Hold a chair for balance. Lift one leg out to the side. Strengthens hips and improves balance.

  • Gentle Stretching Routine 10 minutes

    Neck rolls, shoulder stretches, hamstring stretch, hip flexor stretch, calf stretch. Hold each 20-30 seconds.

Intermediate — Building Strength & Endurance

4 days per week, 30-40 minutes. Light dumbbells or resistance bands recommended.

Day 1 — Lower Body Power

  • Goblet Squats 4 sets x 12 reps

    Hold a dumbbell at chest height. Squat deep with control. Builds quad and glute strength.

  • Romanian Deadlifts 3 sets x 10 reps

    Hold dumbbells, hinge at the hips, keep back flat. Strengthens the entire posterior chain.

  • Reverse Lunges 3 sets x 10 reps each leg

    Step backward into lunge position. Easier on knees than forward lunges.

  • Single-Leg Glute Bridge 3 sets x 10 reps each side

    Same as glute bridge but with one leg extended. Builds balance and isolates each glute.

Day 2 — Upper Body Strength

  • Dumbbell Chest Press 3 sets x 10 reps

    Lie on a bench or floor. Press dumbbells up from chest level. Builds chest and arm strength.

  • Bent-Over Rows 3 sets x 12 reps

    Hinge forward at hips, pull dumbbells to waist. Strengthens upper back and posture.

  • Overhead Press 3 sets x 10 reps

    Press dumbbells overhead from shoulder height. Great for shoulder strength and bone density.

  • Resistance Band Pull-Aparts 3 sets x 15 reps

    Hold band at chest height, pull apart. Improves posture and shoulder health.

Day 3 — Core & Stability

  • Plank Hold 3 sets x 30-45 seconds

    On forearms and toes, body in a straight line. Builds deep core strength.

  • Pallof Press 3 sets x 10 reps each side

    Use a resistance band anchored to your side. Press forward resisting rotation. Excellent anti-rotation core work.

  • Side Plank 3 sets x 20-30 seconds each side

    On forearm and stacked feet. Modify by dropping the bottom knee. Strengthens obliques.

  • Farmer's Carry 3 sets x 30 seconds

    Hold a weight in each hand, walk with tall posture. Builds grip, core, and overall stability.

Day 4 — Active Recovery & Mobility

  • 30-Minute Walk Moderate pace

    Outdoors if possible. Walking is one of the best forms of exercise for longevity and mood.

  • Hip Opener Sequence 10 minutes

    Pigeon stretch, 90/90 stretch, hip circles. Counteracts sitting and keeps hips mobile.

  • Thoracic Spine Rotation 3 sets x 8 reps each side

    On hands and knees, hand behind head, rotate open. Improves upper back mobility.

Advanced — Peak Performance

5 days per week, 40-50 minutes. Dumbbells, resistance bands, and optional barbell.

Day 1 — Heavy Lower Body

  • Barbell or Dumbbell Squats 4 sets x 8 reps

    Focus on depth and control. Heavy squats build bone density and functional strength.

  • Bulgarian Split Squats 3 sets x 10 reps each leg

    Rear foot elevated on bench. Hold dumbbells for added resistance.

  • Hip Thrusts 4 sets x 10 reps

    Back on bench, barbell or heavy dumbbell on hips. The gold standard for glute development.

  • Nordic Hamstring Curl (Assisted) 3 sets x 6 reps

    Kneel with feet anchored, lower slowly. Use hands to push back up as needed.

Day 2 — Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps)

  • Push-Ups (Full or Incline) 4 sets x 12 reps

    Full range of motion. One of the best functional exercises at any age.

  • Dumbbell Shoulder Press 4 sets x 10 reps

    Seated or standing. Press overhead with control.

  • Tricep Dips (Bench) 3 sets x 12 reps

    Hands on bench edge, lower and press. Skip if you have shoulder issues — do tricep kickbacks instead.

  • Lateral Raises 3 sets x 12 reps

    Light weights, lift to shoulder height. Builds shoulder cap definition.

Day 3 — Pull (Back, Biceps)

  • Dumbbell Rows 4 sets x 10 reps each arm

    One hand on bench, row dumbbell to hip. Squeeze shoulder blade at the top.

  • Face Pulls (Band) 3 sets x 15 reps

    Pull band to face level, externally rotating shoulders. Protects shoulder health.

  • Bicep Curls 3 sets x 12 reps

    Dumbbells or resistance band. Slow and controlled.

  • Reverse Flyes 3 sets x 12 reps

    Bent forward, lift light dumbbells to the sides. Builds upper back and improves posture.

Day 4 — Core & Conditioning

  • Turkish Get-Up 3 sets x 3 reps each side

    Full body movement from lying to standing while holding a weight overhead. Master with no weight first.

  • Hanging Knee Raises 3 sets x 10 reps

    Hang from a bar, bring knees to chest. Modify by doing captain's chair or lying leg raises.

  • Kettlebell Swings 4 sets x 15 reps

    Hip hinge movement. Builds explosive hip power and cardiovascular fitness.

  • Ab Rollout (Wheel or Ball) 3 sets x 8 reps

    Kneel, roll forward, pull back with core. Advanced core strength builder.

Day 5 — Active Recovery & Yoga Flow

  • 20-Minute Yoga Flow Sun salutations + warrior sequence

    Focus on breath-linked movement. Builds flexibility, balance, and mental clarity.

  • Foam Rolling 10 minutes

    Roll quads, hamstrings, glutes, upper back. Spend extra time on tight spots.

  • Breathwork 5 minutes

    Box breathing: 4 counts in, 4 counts hold, 4 counts out, 4 counts hold. Reduces cortisol.

Nutrition Guide

Eating well after 40 isn't about restriction — it's about fueling your body with what it needs to thrive. Here are evidence-based guidelines.

🥩 Protein Priority

After 40, muscle protein synthesis slows down. Aim for 1.2-1.6g of protein per kg of body weight daily to maintain and build muscle.

  • Include protein at every meal (25-30g per meal)
  • Great sources: eggs, chicken, fish, Greek yogurt, lentils, tofu
  • Consider a protein shake after workouts if you struggle to hit targets

🦴 Bone-Building Nutrients

Estrogen decline accelerates bone loss. These nutrients are your allies:

  • Calcium: 1,000-1,200mg/day — dairy, leafy greens, fortified foods
  • Vitamin D: 1,000-2,000 IU/day — sunlight, fatty fish, supplements
  • Magnesium: 320mg/day — nuts, seeds, dark chocolate, avocado
  • Vitamin K2: Helps direct calcium to bones — fermented foods, egg yolks

🔥 Managing Metabolism

Metabolic rate naturally decreases with age, but you can work with it:

  • Don't slash calories — under-eating slows metabolism further
  • Eat enough to fuel your workouts (at least 1,600-1,800 cal for active women)
  • Prioritize fiber-rich foods for satiety (25-30g/day)
  • Stay hydrated — 8+ glasses of water daily

🌿 Anti-Inflammatory Foods

Chronic inflammation increases with age. An anti-inflammatory diet can reduce joint pain and improve recovery:

  • Omega-3s: Salmon, sardines, walnuts, flaxseed
  • Berries: Blueberries, strawberries — packed with antioxidants
  • Turmeric & Ginger: Add to smoothies, teas, or cooking
  • Leafy Greens: Spinach, kale, swiss chard
  • Limit: Processed foods, excess sugar, refined oils

🍵 Hormone-Supporting Foods

Certain foods can help ease hormonal transition symptoms:

  • Phytoestrogens: Soy, flaxseed, sesame seeds
  • Cruciferous vegetables: Broccoli, cauliflower — support estrogen metabolism
  • Healthy fats: Avocado, olive oil, nuts — support hormone production
  • B vitamins: Whole grains, eggs, legumes — energy and mood support

Wellness Guide

Fitness is more than exercise. These evidence-based tips address the unique wellness needs of women over 40.

😴 Sleep & Recovery

Sleep quality often declines during perimenopause and menopause. Yet sleep is when your body repairs muscle and regulates hormones.

  • Aim for 7-9 hours per night
  • Keep your bedroom cool (65-68°F / 18-20°C) — especially helpful for night sweats
  • Limit caffeine after 2 PM
  • Try magnesium glycinate before bed — may improve sleep quality
  • Establish a wind-down routine: no screens 30 min before bed

🧠 Stress & Cortisol Management

Chronic stress raises cortisol, which promotes belly fat storage and disrupts sleep. Managing stress is as important as any workout.

  • Practice 5-10 minutes of daily breathwork or meditation
  • Try "physiological sighing" — double inhale through nose, long exhale through mouth
  • Limit high-intensity workouts to 3x/week — too much can raise cortisol
  • Prioritize nature walks — shown to reduce cortisol by 12-16%
  • Say no to things that drain you — rest is productive

🏋️ Strength Training & Bone Health

After 40, women can lose up to 1-2% of bone density per year. Strength training is the most effective countermeasure.

  • Lift heavy enough that the last 2 reps are challenging
  • Focus on compound movements: squats, deadlifts, presses
  • Impact exercises (jumping, stair climbing) also build bone — if joints allow
  • Consistency matters more than intensity — 3x/week is enough
  • Get a DEXA scan to know your baseline bone density

🌡️ Navigating Perimenopause & Menopause

Hormonal changes can affect energy, mood, weight, and recovery. Understanding what's happening helps you train smarter.

  • Hot flashes? Exercise actually reduces their frequency and severity
  • Energy dips are normal — adjust workout intensity day by day
  • Weight may redistribute to the midsection — this is hormonal, not a failure
  • Talk to your doctor about hormone therapy if symptoms are severe
  • Track your cycle (if still menstruating) to plan harder and easier training days

💓 Heart Health

Heart disease risk increases after menopause as estrogen's protective effect decreases. Exercise is your best prevention.

  • Aim for 150 minutes of moderate cardio per week (walking counts!)
  • Add 2+ strength training sessions for heart-protective benefits
  • Monitor blood pressure — know your numbers
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support cardiovascular health
  • Reduce sodium and increase potassium-rich foods

🌞 Mental Health & Community

Physical activity is one of the most effective treatments for anxiety and depression — and community amplifies the effect.

  • Exercise releases endorphins, serotonin, and BDNF (brain growth factor)
  • Find a workout buddy or join a group — accountability helps consistency
  • Celebrate non-scale victories: energy, strength, mood, sleep quality
  • Journal your progress — writing reinforces positive change
  • Be patient with yourself — this is a marathon, not a sprint
